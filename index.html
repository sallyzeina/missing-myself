Case Background

This case does not involve a kidnapping.

No one took me. No one forced me into a different life. That's what makes it difficult to solve.

When I was younger, I never thought much about myself. I wore clothes because I liked them. I spoke when I had something to say. I laughed loudly when something was funny. I cried when I was hurt. I wasn't constantly wondering how I looked while doing those things.

Then I got older.

I learned that some girls were called pretty and some weren't. I learned that being skinny was never officially required, but somehow everyone knew it mattered. I learned that confidence was attractive, but only in the right amount. I learned that speaking up could make people respect you, unless it made them uncomfortable. I learned that there were clothes that looked good on me and clothes that looked "too much."

I learned that there was always a right amount of everything. Too loud. Too emotional. Too confident. Too ambitious. Too sensitive. Too much.

Nobody handed me these rules.

I absorbed them.

From friends. From family. From strangers. From teachers. From comments. From social media.

The strange thing is that every adjustment felt reasonable.

A different outfit. A quieter opinion. A harsh diet. Changing my hair color. Lip fillers. and so on...

Every change was small enough to seem harmless.

But one day I started wondering something.

If every version of me is edited before anyone else sees it, then who exactly are they seeing?

This investigation was opened after the disappearance of a person known as "Myself."

Last confirmed sighting: Before I started asking permission to be who I already was.

What the investigators think

Judith Butler

Butler believed that gender is not something we simply are, but something we learn to do through repeated actions, habits, and expectations.

if I spent years changing parts of myself to fit those expectations, where do those performances end and where do I begin?

Source: Gender Trouble (1990).

Simone de Beauvoir

Butler argues that gender is produced through repeated acts and behaviors that eventually come to feel natural.

if so much of what I believed about myself was learned, what parts of me existed before I learned them?

Source: The Second Sex (1949).

Final Report

Case Number: 0001

Subject: Myself

Date Opened: Unknown. Investigators were unable to determine when the disappearance began.

Cause of Disappearance: No single event was identified. Evidence suggests the disappearance occurred gradually through comparisons, expectations, self-doubt, and the pressure to become someone easier to admire than to understand.

Persons of Interest: Beauty standards, gender expectations, social media, comments disguised as compliments, unwritten rules, and the belief that being different versions of myself would eventually make me enough.

Investigation Findings: The missing person was not lost all at once. Small pieces disappeared over many years. An outfit left unworn. A photograph deleted. An opinion kept to myself. A number on a scale given more importance than it deserved.

Theoretical Analysis: Guided by the work of Judith Butler and Simone de Beauvoir, investigators found that many of the expectations shaping the subject's identity were learned over time until they began to feel natural.

The case revealed that the missing person was never completely gone.

She was hiding underneath years of expectations, comparison, and performance.

Recovery remains ongoing.
